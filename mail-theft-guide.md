# Mail Theft Response & Prevention Guide
*A practical, step-by-step guide for victims of stolen mail (U.S.-focused, with international equivalents)*

> **Why this matters:** Mail theft is a federal crime (18 U.S.C. § 1708) punishable by fines and up to 5 years in prison. Stolen mail is a leading gateway to identity theft — thieves look for checks, credit/debit cards, bank statements, tax documents, medical cards, passports, and anything with your SSN. Acting fast dramatically limits the damage.

---

## 1) IMMEDIATE STEPS (First 24 hours)

☐ **Confirm it's theft, not delay.** Check USPS Tracking (for packages) or the sender for expected delivery dates. If you have Informed Delivery, compare what was imaged as delivered to what actually arrived.

☐ **Document everything.** Make a list of:
- Date/time you discovered the theft
- What's missing (sender name, type of mail: check #s, card names, account numbers, document types)
- Whether the mailbox was damaged/tampered with
- Take photos of any damaged mailbox or lock

☐ **Call 911 if you see theft in progress** (someone at a mailbox or collection box now).

☐ **Contact senders of high-risk items immediately** (see Section 5 for details):
- Bank — if checks or checkbooks were stolen
- Card issuer — if a credit/debit card was in transit
- IRS / benefits agency — if tax refunds, benefits, or government checks were stolen
- Employer/pension — if payroll or benefit checks were stolen

☐ **Report to the U.S. Postal Inspection Service** (Section 2) and **file a police report** (Section 5).

☐ **Change online banking/USPS.com passwords** if any account notices or PIN mailers were in the stolen mail.

## 2) REPORTING MAIL THEFT

### United States
| Channel | How |
|---|---|
| **U.S. Postal Inspection Service (USPIS)** — the federal law enforcement arm for mail crime | Online: **uspis.gov/report** (choose "Mail Theft"). Phone: **1-877-876-2455** and follow the prompts (mail theft / "Emergency" for dangerous or suspicious mail) |
| **Crime in progress** | Call **911** first, then USPIS |
| **Local police** | File a report (needed by banks/creditors; see Section 5). Get the report number and a copy |
| **USPS customer service (mail delivery problems)** | **1-800-ASK-USPS (1-800-275-8777)** or your local post office; USPS also runs a free **Missing Mail Search** at usps.com/help/missing-mail.htm |
| **Insurance claims** (insured/lost packages) | File within **60 days** of mailing via usps.com |
| **If you suspect a USPS employee** | USPS Office of Inspector General: **1-888-USPS-OIG (1-888-877-7644)** or uspoig.gov |
| **Mail fraud (scams via mail)** | uspis.gov/report → "Mail Fraud," or PS Form 8165 |
| **Scam emails/texts pretending to be USPS** | Forward to **spam@uspis.gov**, then delete |

**Tip:** Save your USPIS report confirmation — some banks and the credit bureaus (for extended fraud alerts) will ask for proof of the crime.

### Equivalents in other countries
- **Canada:** Canada Post customer service (1-866-607-6301) for missing mail; report theft/fraud to your local police and the **Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501)**.
- **UK:** **Royal Mail** (missing/damaged mail) and **Action Fraud** (actionfraud.police.uk, 0300 123 2040) for theft/fraud.
- **Australia:** **Australia Post** for missing mail; report cyber/fraud crime via **ReportCyber (cyber.gov.au)** and Scamwatch.

## 3) PROTECT AGAINST IDENTITY THEFT & FRAUD FROM STOLEN MAIL

### If **checks or a checkbook** were stolen
1. Call your bank's **fraud department immediately** (number on your bank statement/website — not from the stolen mail).
2. **Place stop-payment orders** on any missing checks (banks may charge a small fee; stop orders typically last ~6 months–1 year).
3. If a **checkbook or blank check stock** was stolen, ask about **closing the account and opening a new one** — this is the only permanent fix, since thieves can chemically "wash" and alter checks.
4. Review statements for checks you didn't write; file a fraud claim for any that cleared.
5. Consider reporting to **ChexSystems** (consumer credit report for bank accounts) if new-account fraud occurs.

### If a **credit or debit card** was stolen (or one you were expecting never arrived)
1. **Report to the issuer immediately** and have the card cancelled and reissued. Cards reported lost/stolen before any fraudulent charges: **$0 liability**; credit card liability is capped at **$50** under the Fair Credit Billing Act; most issuers' zero-liability policies cover even that. (Debit cards: report within **2 business days** to keep liability at $0–$50; waiting up to 60 days can cost up to $500 or more.)
2. If a card **you never received** shows up on a statement as activated or charged — report it as fraud; the issuer will reissue.
3. Monitor statements daily for several months afterward.

### If **identity theft is suspected or confirmed**
1. Go to **IdentityTheft.gov** (FTC) — or call **1-877-438-4338**. It generates an official **Identity Theft Report** and a personalized recovery plan. This report is your key document for banks, bureaus, and agencies.
2. **Place a fraud alert with ONE bureau — they must notify the other two** (free):
   - **Equifax: 1-800-525-6285**
   - **Experian: 1-888-397-3742**
   - **TransUnion: 1-800-680-7285**
   - Initial alert: **1 year**. **Extended 7-year alert** (best if ID theft is confirmed) requires your Identity Theft Report or police report.
3. **Consider a credit freeze at all three bureaus** (free, permanent until you lift it; strongest protection — blocks new credit):
   - Equifax: 1-888-378-4329 (freeze line) or myEquifax.com
   - Experian: 1-888-397-3742
   - TransUnion: 1-888-909-8872
4. **Pull your credit reports free** at **AnnualCreditReport.com** and dispute anything fraudulent.
5. Close or flag any **accounts opened fraudulently**; get written confirmation of closure.

### If **government documents** were stolen
| Document | Action |
|---|---|
| **Social Security card** | Replace free via **ssa.gov** (my Social Security account) or Form **SS-5** at an SSA office (limit ~3/year, 10/lifetime). If the **number** is being misused: SSA fraud hotline **1-800-269-0271** + FTC report; you can also block electronic access to your SSN record at ssa.gov |
| **IRS refund check / tax documents** | Refund check missing/stolen: file **Form 3911** ("Taxpayer Statement Regarding Refund") to start a refund trace — IRS cancels and reissues. If your SSN was used for tax fraud: file **Form 14039** (Identity Theft Affidavit) and request an **IP PIN** (Identity Protection PIN) at irs.gov |
| **U.S. passport** | Report via **Form DS-64** online at travel.state.gov (fastest — passport cancelled within 1 business day) or call **1-877-487-2778**. Replace in person with DS-11 + DS-64 |
| **Driver's license / state ID** | Contact your state DMV; a police report is usually required for replacement when stolen |
| **Medicare/Medicaid card** | Call **1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227)**; report misuse of your Medicare number |
| **Permanent resident card (green card)/EAD/work permit** | Report to USCIS and request replacement (Form I-90) |

### Ongoing monitoring (next 6–12 months)
- Review bank/card statements and credit reports monthly; watch for unfamiliar accounts, hard inquiries, address changes.
- Watch for IRS "more than one return filed" notices, benefit statements you didn't request, medical bills for care you didn't receive.
- Keep copies of all reports (USPIS, police, FTC) in one folder — you'll be asked for them repeatedly.

## 4) PREVENTIVE MEASURES FOR THE FUTURE

**USPS tools (all free)**
- **Informed Delivery** (informeddelivery.usps.com): daily email with grayscale images of incoming letter mail + package tracking. You'll know what's supposed to arrive each day — so you can spot missing checks/cards *immediately*. Note: a thief can attempt to sign up in your name, so if you're unexpectedly locked out, check it — an unknown signup can itself be a red flag.
- **USPS Hold Mail** (usps.com/manage/hold-mail.htm): pauses delivery at the Post Office for **3–30 days** while you travel (request up to 30 days ahead).
- **Hold for Pickup / Signature Confirmation**: have valuable or sensitive mail held at the Post Office or require a signature.
- **Registered Mail** for high-value items (insured and chain-of-custody tracked).
- **P.O. Box or a USPS-approved locking mailbox** if you're in a theft-prone area — cluster boxes and locked boxes cut off casual theft.

**Daily habits (per Postal Inspection Service)**
- **Retrieve mail promptly**; never leave it in the box or at the door overnight.
- **Deposit outgoing mail close to pickup time** — check the posted schedule on blue collection boxes; never leave outgoing mail in your box with the flag up overnight. Hand mail directly to the carrier or use the slot **inside** the Post Office.
- **Don't send cash**; minimize mailing checks (pay electronically when possible).
- If an expected check, card, or refund **doesn't arrive**, **contact the sender immediately** — that's often the first sign of theft.
- Ask a trusted neighbor/friend to collect mail when you're away (plus Hold Mail).
- **Shred** junk mail, pre-approved offers, and old documents before discarding.
- **Opt out of prescreened credit offers**: optoutprescreen.com or **1-888-5-OPT-OUT** (1-888-567-8688) — stops most credit-card/benefit offers from ever hitting your box.
- Switch to **paperless statements** and **direct deposit** for paychecks, tax refunds, Social Security, and pension payments (so nothing valuable sits in a box).
- **File a change of address** with USPS *and* notify banks/cards whenever you move — misdelivered mail at old addresses is a classic theft source.
- Consider a **doorbell/porch camera** with a view of the mailbox, and report suspicious people near boxes/collection boxes to police and USPIS.
- Enroll in your bank/card issuers' **transaction alerts** (text/email for every charge) for instant fraud detection.

## 5) WHEN TO CONTACT POLICE OR FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

### Police — call/file a report when:
- You **witness theft in progress** (call **911** immediately).
- Mail was **stolen** (not merely lost) — especially if it contained checks, cards, IDs, tax documents, or prescriptions. Even if police only note it, a report creates a record; USPIS investigates the federal offense.
- You discover **actual or attempted identity theft** — creditors, banks, and credit bureaus frequently **require a police report** to: remove fraudulent accounts, process fraud claims, replace government IDs, and grant 7-year extended fraud alerts. Credit bureaus will block fraudulent items from your credit report when you provide a police report. Get the **report number** and a copy.
- Your **mailbox was broken into or vandalized**.

### Financial institutions — contact immediately when:
- **Checks** were stolen → stop payments / account closure (same day).
- A **card** was stolen or an expected card didn't arrive → cancel + reissue (same day). Remember the liability windows: debit card protections are time-sensitive (**report within 2 business days**), and billing-error disputes generally must be raised within **60 days** of the statement.
- **Account statements, new-card PINs, or welcome kits** were stolen → ask the bank to flag your account, reissue cards, change online credentials, and add extra verification.
- You see **any unauthorized transaction** → report in writing and keep the confirmation; ask for the fraud claim reference number.
- Stolen mail included **mortgage/loan documents, medical statements, or investment info** → notify those institutions so they can add security flags.

## TL;DR CHECKLIST
1. **Secure the money first:** stop payments/cancel cards/close accounts (hours matter, especially for debit cards).
2. **Report:** USPIS online or 1-877-876-2455; police report for theft/ID fraud; 911 if in progress.
3. **Lock your credit:** fraud alert (one bureau) or freeze (all three); report at IdentityTheft.gov.
4. **Replace stolen documents:** bank checks, cards, passport (DS-64), SS card (SS-5), IRS (3911/14039 + IP PIN), DMV.
5. **Prevent a repeat:** Informed Delivery, locking mailbox/P.O. box, Hold Mail when away, prompt pickup, mail near pickup time, opt-out of prescreened offers, direct deposit + paperless, shred everything.
